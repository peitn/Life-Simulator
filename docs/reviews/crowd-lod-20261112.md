# Crowd level of detail, step 1 (12 November 2026)

Run `main-claude-1794511294-343272`. Goal: the first working level of detail (LOD) for characters, which the
11 November budget review made a precondition for eight characters with dense hair and for the web export.

## Starting point

There was no LOD at all. `character.glb` is loaded at runtime through `GLTFDocument`, which does not generate the
LODs that the editor importer would. Every character on screen therefore drew its full face (12,560 triangles,
90 blend shapes), eyes, teeth, lashes and up to 40,000 triangles of strand cards, at any distance.

## What was built

### 1. Geometry LODs (`scripts/assets/character_lod.gd`)

- `CharacterLod.with_lods(mesh)` rebuilds an `ArrayMesh` with automatic index LODs from
  `ImporterMesh.generate_lods` (meshoptimizer). The LODs only add index buffers, so vertices, blend shapes
  (face shape and expressions), skin weights and the custom `CUSTOM0` rest positions used by the skin shader are
  unchanged. Godot picks the level by screen-space error.
- A LOD is kept only if the welded border edge set is exactly the same as the full mesh. Otherwise that level and
  all coarser ones are dropped. This protects the head/neck seam and the openings for the eyes, lips and mouth from
  gaps. On the current model only the two coarsest body levels were dropped.
- Meshes under 600 triangles (lashes, lips, tongue, gums, iris) and non-indexed or non-triangle meshes are left
  alone.
- `AssetLibrary` calls it once when a GLB is loaded (after the rest positions are added), so all characters share
  the same LOD meshes. Cost: about 150 ms once per game for the character model.
- Results: face 12,560 → 6,280 / 3,140 / 1,570 / 784 / 392 / 196 triangles; 9 meshes of the character get LODs.
- The cache holds only weak references to the source and the result (a Gemini finding: the first version kept the
  result alive forever).

### 2. Character detail tiers (`SimVisual`)

The tier comes from the share of the screen height a 1.8 m figure takes in the active camera of the character's
viewport (perspective depth along the view axis, or the orthographic size), so it does not depend on resolution
or zoom. Going away switches at the threshold, coming closer needs 15 % more (hysteresis). Each character checks
4 times a second with a phase spread by instance, and immediately on its first frame.

| Tier | Screen share | Changes |
|------|--------------|---------|
| 0 | ≥ 0.20 | Everything. |
| 1 | 0.055–0.20 | Expressions computed at 10 Hz with the summed time (blinks and speech still run); eyes, lashes, mouth, brow and beard cards cast no shadows. |
| 2 | < 0.055 | Strand cards (hair, brows, beard) hidden, the low-poly GLB "helmet" for the style shown instead (it already carries the colour match used while cards build); mouth interior hidden; expression frozen with eyes open (only an emotion's lid droop) and the jaw closed. |

With the game camera (FOV 50°, 18 m default) a character takes about 0.107 of the screen height, so it is in
tier 1; tier 2 starts at about 35 m. No camera (dedicated server, headless runs) means tier 0, so nothing changes
there. `lod_override` forces a tier for previews and tests. Nothing is replicated: this is client presentation only.

### 3. Setting

`GameSettings.crowd_detail` (0 low, 1 medium – default, 2 high) multiplies the screen share by 0.6 / 1.0 / 1.6.
The settings menu has a "Detail postav v dálce" option. Invalid values (text, NaN, infinity) are rejected,
others are clamped.

## Verification

- `tests/test_character_lod.gd` **76/76**: tier logic, hysteresis and oscillation, camera share; GLB with and
  without LODs compared field by field (positions, UV, bones, weights, `CUSTOM0`, LOD0 indices identical;
  normals and tangents within the 16-bit octahedral quantisation of any `ArrayMesh` rebuild); blend shape names,
  mode and data identical; strictly decreasing triangle counts; the border of every LOD checked independently
  from the index buffer of the `RenderingServer`; sharing between characters; cache without a leak; synthetic
  edge cases; `SimVisual` with a real camera at 3 m, 16 m and 64 m, back again, look change in the crowd, forced
  tier, no camera, bald character, the setting and its validation; switch 0.1 ms, check 3 µs.
- Regression of 27 suites, including the GPU placeholder render test under Xvfb (`test_h3_async_placeholder_render`
  78/78) – all passed except the known `test_assets` 154/155 (sitting pelvis height) and `test_skin` 60/61.
  `test_mp_reconnect` failed once when run in parallel with three other Godot processes (handshake timing) and
  passed 72/72 alone.
- Render (`tools/render_crowd_lod.tscn`, GL Compatibility under Xvfb):
  `docs/images/crowd-lod-levels-20261112.jpg` – full face and LOD 1–3 with edges; the silhouette, nose and jaw stay
  readable down to 1,570 triangles and the eye, lip and neck borders do not open.
  `docs/images/crowd-lod-tiers-20261112.jpg` – tier 0 and tier 2 of two characters seen from close up.

## Known limits

- The helmet is the same two shells for every style. Close up it looks nothing like long wavy or curly hair (see
  the tiers image); it is meant only for characters that are a small part of the screen, but voluminous styles
  will visibly change outline when they cross the tier 2 threshold. Next step: a per-style low-poly hair proxy
  generated from the HairRig geometry.
- Strand cards and procedural details (hands, feet, torso, C5 garments) have no LODs yet. Blend shapes are not
  pruned: skinning and blend-shape cost per vertex is unchanged, only the drawn triangles drop.
- Tier switches are instant (no dither fade). Expressions at 10 Hz in tier 1 are not interpolated in between.
- No measurement on a real GPU or in the web export yet.

## Gemini audit

`gemini-3.5-flash` was overloaded; `flash-lite` answered, but the connector cut most answers short. Accepted:
the result cache kept strong references (fixed). Rejected with reasons: moving the 150 ms generation to a thread
(it runs once inside the already synchronous GLB load), per-character camera lookups (3 µs per check, 4 per
second), "add time slicing" (already spread by instance id), shadow loss on eyes and lashes in tier 1 (they are a
few pixels at that size). Noted for later: dither fade on tier switches and interpolation of 10 Hz expressions.
